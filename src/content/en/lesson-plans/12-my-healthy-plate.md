## Objective

Students can sort everyday foods into the four groups of a balanced plate and assemble a meal that is both healthy and varied.

## Preparation

- Printed worksheet, one per student (five tasks, plus a strip of food pictures to cut out along the bottom edge).
- Coloured pencils for the first task, scissors and glue for the fourth.
- A glass of water on the teacher's desk — the material returns to drinking water at every step.
- Optional: an enlarged copy of the plate diagram for the board, so the four groups can be filled in together.

## Lesson flow

1. **What is healthy?** · 7 min — Students mark ten everyday items with a tick or a cross: apple, crisps, broccoli, fizzy drink, water, cake, carrot, sweets, yoghurt, pizza. Two or three students explain a decision they found hard.
2. **The healthy plate** · 10 min — Students look at the plate divided into four parts — vegetables, grains, fruit, proteins, with water in the middle — and write into each part which foods belong there. The class works through the first group together, the remaining three independently.
3. **Choose the healthier meal** · 8 min — Five pairs are compared and the healthier option circled: salad or a burger with chips, water or a fizzy drink, porridge or chocolate cereal, a sandwich or crisps, fruit or ice cream. Each choice is justified in one sentence.
4. **Build your own plate** · 15 min — Students cut out the food pictures from the bottom strip and glue them onto the empty plate so that the meal is healthy and varied — and do not forget the glass of water. The finished plates are laid out on the desks and the class walks past to see them.
5. **Mini quiz and the golden rules** · 5 min — Four multiple-choice questions: how many meals a day are recommended; what gives us energy; which food is a good source of protein; how much water we should drink a day. The lesson closes with the five golden rules: eat varied food, eat more fruit and vegetables, drink plenty of water, eat fewer sweets, crisps and fizzy drinks, be active every day.

## Learning outcomes

- Students can tell foods that support health from those that should be eaten rarely.
- Students can name the four groups on a balanced plate and give an example food for each.
- Students can choose the healthier option in a pair of everyday meals and justify the choice.
- Students can assemble a meal containing all four groups and a drink of water.
- Students can name at least three rules of healthy eating.

## Notes

- The lesson is cross-curricular: sorting and justifying belong to Science and Society, while the healthy-habits part belongs to Physical and Health Education. It also works as a themed day in an after-school club.
- Check the quiz answers against the health-education material the school actually uses before printing. Recommendations on meals and water intake vary by source, and this material was generated with an AI tool.
- The cut-and-glue task is the part younger students remember. Allow the full fifteen minutes for it; if time is short, cut the third task rather than this one.
- The material was created with ChatGPT and is published for reuse. Students do not use an AI tool in this lesson.
