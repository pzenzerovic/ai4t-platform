## Objective

Students show that they have read and understood the novel through four different routes — a written worksheet, work with characters, a team escape room and a code-breaking task — so that both the strong readers and the reluctant ones have a way in.

## Preparation

- Print the main comprehension worksheet (or the shorter nine-task version for a class that needs less) — one per student.
- Print and cut the eight character cards; one set per group of 4–5 students.
- Print the escape room sheet; prepare answer slips and decide the station layout in the classroom.
- Print the secret-message sheet for the students who will use it as an extension.
- Check the character names and any quotations against the translation the class is actually reading before printing.

## Lesson flow

1. **Lesson 1 · 45 min — comprehension worksheet** — Twelve tasks covering the whole novel: who is who; the place and time of the action; characterisation of the characters; good against bad behaviour; the chronology of events; true/false statements; discussion questions; matching concepts; short answers; a secret message to decode; a creative task (draw a scene); and a bonus question. The sheet closes with a glossary of supporting vocabulary (grund, loyalty, friendship, betrayal) for students who need help with the words rather than with the story.
2. **Lesson 2 · 45 min — the characters** — Groups work with the eight character cards: Nemeček, Boka Janoš, Gereb, Ferenc Ač, the Pásztors, Čele, Slavko and professor Rácz. Each card carries an illustration, a short description of character and role, a symbol and a line typical of that character. Activities: who is this, describe the character, role play, match the character to the description, and a group discussion about them.
3. **Lesson 3 · 45 min — escape room "Defend the grund!"** — Seven stations, each solved task giving one letter of the final code: 1 who is who (matching characters to descriptions); 2 the chronological order of events; 3 a secret message to decode; 4 Gereb's betrayal (crossword); 5 the battle for the grund (the rules of chivalry); 6 Nemeček's courage; 7 punishments and rules (rebus). The final code unlocks the message that the grund has been defended. The source allows 30–45 minutes for this material; the remaining time goes to the closing discussion.
4. **Extension (outside the three lessons) — secret messages** — Six types of cipher inspired by the children's secrecy in the novel: letter-for-number substitution, symbol substitution, mirror writing, missing letters, a number cipher, and a rebus set in the botanical garden. Used as a bonus for faster students or as an advanced round once the novel has been covered.

## Learning outcomes

- Students can name the main characters and describe the role of each in the plot.
- Students can put the key events of the novel in chronological order.
- Students can distinguish loyalty from betrayal using examples from the text and explain the difference in their own words.
- Students can justify a judgement about a character's behaviour with an event from the novel.
- Students can solve a task that requires combining knowledge of the text with logical reasoning.

## Notes

- The four materials are designed as one whole and are kept as one plan. A single worksheet leaves the strong readers bored after fifteen minutes and lets the non-readers pretend; the pack gives each group a different way in.
- The teacher chooses which materials to use: the shorter worksheet version instead of the full one, the escape room instead of a second written lesson, the ciphers only for those who want them.
- The same structure transfers to any other set reading — the pack is a pattern, not a one-off.
- Check the character names, the spelling of names and any quotations against the translation used in class. AI tools mix up characters from similar novels, and this is exactly the kind of material where that shows.
- The material was created with ChatGPT and is published for reuse. Students do not use an AI tool in these lessons.
