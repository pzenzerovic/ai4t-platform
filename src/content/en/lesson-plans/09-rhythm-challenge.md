## Objective

Students can repeat a heard rhythm with clapping or body percussion and write it down using agreed symbols, and can invent a rhythm of their own.

## Preparation

- Printed worksheet, one per student (five listening tasks, "invent your own rhythm", "the challenge", and the symbol legend at the bottom).
- The four audio files downloaded in advance and ready to play, in this order: basic clap (an easy 4/4 warm-up), fast rhythm, slow rhythm, mixed rhythm.
- A speaker loud enough for the whole room; check the volume before the lesson.
- Enough space for students to clap, stamp and snap without hitting a neighbour.
- The symbol legend written large on the board: clap, foot, chest, snap (fingers), pause.

## Lesson flow

1. **What is rhythm, and how do we write it down** · 5 min — Rhythm is a pattern of long and short sounds; we feel it with the body, with clapping and with movement. The teacher introduces the five symbols from the legend and the class practises each one once.
2. **Listen — repeat — write, tasks 1 to 3** · 15 min — For each task the same cycle: the teacher plays the sound (or performs the rhythm), students repeat it with clapping or body percussion, then write it with symbols in the worksheet. Tasks 1 to 3 stay in the easier range, building from clapping only to clap-and-foot combinations.
3. **Tasks 4 and 5** · 10 min — The same cycle with the longer and faster patterns, where the pause and the snap enter the sequence. Students who cannot yet write the pattern down keep repeating it aloud with the group first.
4. **Invent your own rhythm** · 10 min — Students invent their own rhythm, write it with the symbols in the box provided, and perform it for a partner, who has to repeat it back.
5. **The challenge and closing** · 5 min — Students perform their own rhythm again but faster, slower, louder or quieter, and name what changed. Closing point: the same pattern sounds like a different piece of music when the tempo and dynamics change.

## Learning outcomes

- Students can repeat a heard rhythmic pattern with clapping or body percussion.
- Students can write a heard rhythm down using the agreed symbols.
- Students can distinguish a sound from a pause within a rhythmic pattern.
- Students can invent and notate a rhythm of their own and perform it for others.
- Students can perform the same rhythm at a different tempo and dynamic and describe the difference.

## Notes

- The four audio files were generated with an AI tool, together with the worksheet. This is the clearest example in the collection that AI output is not only text — the sound files are part of the teaching material, not an illustration of it.
- Generating audio of this kind requires a paid tier of the tool; the files themselves are published and can simply be downloaded and used.
- If the audio cannot be played, the teacher performs each rhythm live — the worksheet works either way.
- The material was created with ChatGPT and is published for reuse. Students do not use an AI tool in this lesson.
