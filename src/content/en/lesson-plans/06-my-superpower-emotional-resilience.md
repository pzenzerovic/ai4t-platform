## Objective

Students can name what they feel, can replace an unhelpful thought with a supportive one, and can name at least one thing that helps them when something is hard.

## Preparation

- Printed student worksheets, one per student; crayons or markers.
- Emotion cards and pictures of everyday situations, prepared in advance.
- Post-it notes and a box labelled "Worries and superpowers".
- Scissors and glue (optional), a projector (not required).
- Facilitator: school pedagogue, psychologist or the class teacher. Seating in a circle for the opening and the closing round.

## Lesson flow

1. **Emotion traffic light** · 5 min — Red, yellow and green are set out on the board or on the floor. Students stand by, or point to, the colour that matches how they feel today. Discussion questions: what do emotions tell us, are all emotions allowed, can we be sad and brave at the same time?
2. **Guess the emotion** · 10 min — The facilitator shows pictures of emotions, emoji cards and short situations ("a friend did not invite you to a birthday party", "you got a pet", "you forgot your homework"). Students show the emotion with their face, act it out, and describe how the body reacts.
3. **Superhero thoughts** · 10 min — The facilitator reads unhelpful thoughts out loud ("I can't do it", "everyone is better than me", "I always get it wrong"). Students turn each one into a "superhero thought" ("I can try", "mistakes help learning", "nobody is perfect"). The aim is to tell an unhelpful thought from a supportive one.
4. **Good and unhelpful behaviour** · 10 min — Using pictures or behaviour cards, students sort examples: helping a friend, sharing, listening, inviting someone into a game, asking for help — against teasing, pushing, insulting, ignoring others, mocking someone's feelings. Students raise a green circle for good and a red circle for unhelpful behaviour.
5. **My superpower and closing round** · 10 min — Creative part (7 min): students draw their shield of resilience, their superpower and what helps them when things are hard, and may write "I am brave when…", "It helps me when…", "I am proud of myself when…". Closing reflection (3 min): in the circle each student finishes one sentence — "Today I learned…", "I felt…", "My superpower is…".

## Learning outcomes

- Students can recognise and name basic emotions in themselves and in others.
- Students can distinguish a helpful thought from an unhelpful one and can reformulate an unhelpful one.
- Students can distinguish supportive from hurtful behaviour in a group.
- Students can name at least one way of asking for help.
- Students can name a personal strength that helps them when something is difficult.

## Notes

- Facilitator's rules from the source: never judge a feeling as "silly" or "wrong"; never force a student to share a personal experience; use short, simple sentences; praise effort and honesty; make clear that all emotions are allowed, but not all behaviour is acceptable.
- Optional extensions: the "worry balloon" (students write a worry anonymously and put it in the box, then the group thinks of support together); colours of emotions (anger red, sadness blue, calm green, happiness yellow); a calming corner with a stress ball, beanbags, quiet music and breathing cards.
- Messages the workshop is built to send: it is fine to ask for help; emotions are not a weakness; mistakes help learning; nobody is perfect; everyone has their own superpower.
- Content basis: UNICEF's collection of worksheets for developing children's emotional resilience, "Tvoja supermoć". The source material states this explicitly and the attribution must travel with the plan.
