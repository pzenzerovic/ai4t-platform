## Objective

Each participant leaves the workshop with three materials they can use in their own classroom — a quiz, a differentiated worksheet and an interactive game — and with a prompt formula and three safety rules they can apply on their own.

## Preparation

- A laptop or tablet for each participant, with internet access.
- Accounts for at least one general AI tool (ChatGPT or Claude) and access to Google Forms or Microsoft Forms.
- Optional accounts for Wordwall, Kahoot, Padlet or Mentimeter, depending on which game format participants choose.
- The participant materials open on a screen: the A4 handout, the cheat sheet and the Word collection of ready-made prompts by subject.
- Projector for the facilitator; participants seated so that the facilitator can walk between them during the hands-on part.

## Lesson flow

1. **Opening question** · 3 min — How many hours a week do you spend preparing tests, worksheets and written feedback? Participants share their answer with the group. This sets the measure against which the workshop is judged.
2. **The tools** · 9 min — A live tour of five general tools (ChatGPT, Claude, Microsoft Copilot, Google Gemini, NotebookLM) and the specialised ones (MagicSchool.ai, Brisk Teaching, Canva for Education, Diffit.me), each with its strength and its weakness. Participants open one tool and send the same first prompt to compare answers. The prompt formula is introduced here: ROLE + CONTEXT + TASK + FORMAT + CONSTRAINTS.
3. **Three hands-on tasks** · 18 min — Each participant works with content from their own subject: **quiz** (6 min) — generate eight questions, transfer them into Google or Microsoft Forms, mark the correct answers and points; **worksheet** (6 min) — generate a sheet with an introductory text, three levels of difficulty and a bonus task, then ask for a version adapted for a student with learning difficulties; **game** (6 min) — choose one format and paste the generated content into it: matching pairs in Wordwall, a quiz competition in Kahoot, or open discussion questions in Padlet or Mentimeter.
4. **Independent work and sharing** · 12 min — Participants finish their materials while the facilitator circulates and helps. In the last five minutes two or three participants show the group what they made.
5. **Three golden rules** · 3 min — Check everything: names, dates, numbers and quotations. No personal data: never enter students' names, personal identification numbers or diagnoses into public AI tools. Show your students: using AI with critical distance is itself a lesson in digital literacy — demonstrate how it gets things wrong.

## Learning outcomes

- Participants can write a prompt using the five-part formula and improve a weak prompt by adding context and constraints.
- Participants can generate a quiz with an AI tool and turn it into a self-marking form.
- Participants can generate a differentiated worksheet with three levels and request an adapted version for a student with learning difficulties.
- Participants can transfer AI-generated content into an interactive game platform.
- Participants can name the typical types of AI error and check at least one fact before printing.
- Participants can state the rule on personal data in public AI tools.

## Notes

- The workshop's own fact-check example: asked when Marulić's *Judita* was published, the tool answered 1501. It was written in 1501 but printed in 1521 in Venice. A partly correct date presented as a fact — had it gone into a worksheet, students would have learned it wrong.
- Six recurring error types the workshop warns about: dates and years; numbers and statistics; quotations; bibliography; arithmetic (the third step of a calculation); and local context (mixing foreign systems with the Croatian one).
- What is allowed and what is not is set out explicitly: drafting tests, worksheets, adapted texts and messages to parents is fine; entering personal data, printing unchecked content, crediting the AI as an author, and using AI to grade final work are not.
- Take-home materials: the A4 handout, the cheat sheet, the Word collection of about fifty ready-made prompts by subject and role, and the participant page itself.
