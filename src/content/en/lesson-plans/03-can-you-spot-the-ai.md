## Objective

Participants can identify where artificial intelligence is already present in their daily school environment and can tell an AI-powered system apart from a digital tool that only automates a fixed procedure.

## Preparation

- Presentation slides or a printed worksheet with the scenario table.
- Printed scenario cards (optional). The card set is written for the Croatian school context — e-Dnevnik, CARNET, Teams, the school bell.
- A short evaluation form for the end of the session.
- Seating that allows a plenary discussion; scenarios can also be worked in pairs.

## Lesson flow

1. **Introduction** · 5 min — The facilitator opens with the framing: artificial intelligence is already part of everyday teaching practice, often without us consciously noticing. This task helps participants recognise AI in familiar school situations.
2. **Scenario analysis** · 15–30 min — Participants are shown school situations one at a time and decide for each whether AI is being used. After each answer a short explanation follows. The eight scenarios in the source: a spelling tool that predicts the next word (AI — machine learning and language models); an automated bell schedule (not AI — pre-programmed automation without learning); YouTube recommending educational videos (AI — algorithm based on user behaviour); a digital attendance register (not AI — a simple database); speech-to-text in an online class (AI — speech recognition); Google Photos sorting pictures by faces (AI — facial recognition); an interactive whiteboard displaying slides (not AI — display technology); a chatbot answering questions on the school website (AI — natural language processing).
3. **Reflection and discussion** · 10 min — Three questions: which examples surprised you, how often do you consciously think about AI use at school, does AI always mean "intelligent thinking"? The facilitator draws out the difference between automation and AI, the idea of invisible AI, and the point that AI recognises patterns but does not understand the way a human does.

## Learning outcomes

- Participants can recognise the presence of AI in the school environment.
- Participants can distinguish AI systems from standard digital tools.
- Participants can explain that not every technology is AI.
- Participants begin to develop a critical awareness of AI in education.

## Notes

- Alignment with the EU AI Literacy Framework — domain: Engaging with AI; competence: recognising AI systems and understanding how they basically work in real-world contexts.
- Optional extension: each participant lists three examples of AI they believe exist in their own school and explains why they consider them AI-based. This works well as a bridge to the next module.
- The source states a total of 30–45 minutes and fixes the introduction at 5 minutes and the reflection at 10; the length of the scenario analysis is given as the remainder and depends on how much discussion each scenario draws.
