## Objective

Students can turn a situation described in words into an equation with one unknown, solve it, and check whether the result makes sense in the situation it came from.

## Preparation

- Printed worksheet, one per student (seven word problems, a mini quiz and a bonus task).
- Each problem has two lines — one for the equation, one for the solution. Insist on both: writing the equation is the part being practised.
- Board space for modelling the first problem together.
- Optional: ask the class beforehand which apps and shops they use, so the bonus task can be steered towards their own examples.

## Lesson flow

1. **What is an unknown, and how do we write an equation** · 5 min — Short revision: the unknown is the number we are looking for, and an equation is a sentence about that number. The teacher models the first problem on the board and shows how a sentence becomes an equation.
2. **Problems 1 to 3** · 15 min — *Spotify playlist*: the same number of songs each day, 49 in a week plus 7 more on Sunday — how many a day? *Shopping at Zara*: three identical T-shirts cost €54 — how much is one? *Match tickets*: five tickets cost €75 — how much is one? Students write the equation first, then solve.
3. **Problems 4 to 7** · 15 min — *Mobile data*: after using 12 GB, 28 GB remain — how much was there at the start? *Pizza night*: four identical pizzas for €48 — how much is one? *Gaming tokens*: after spending 15 tokens, 33 remain — how many were there at the start? *TikTok challenge*: the same number of views every hour, 3,600 views after six hours — how many per hour?
4. **Mini quiz** · 5 min — Three questions: what is the unknown; what do we do first when solving an equation; if x + 9 = 20, what is x? The quiz checks the procedure rather than the arithmetic.
5. **Bonus: invent your own equation** · 5 min — Students write their own real-life situation (music, shopping, sport, gaming, social media), the equation that goes with it and its solution. Two or three read theirs out and the class solves them.

## Learning outcomes

- Students can translate a situation given in words into an equation with one unknown.
- Students can solve a linear equation with one unknown.
- Students can check a solution by substituting it back into the situation.
- Students can explain what the unknown stands for in a given problem.
- Students can compose their own word problem that leads to a linear equation.

## Notes

- The point of the material is the context: the problems are set in Spotify, Zara, match tickets, mobile data, pizza, gaming tokens and TikTok rather than in apples and pears. That is what makes students read the problem instead of hunting for the numbers.
- Check the arithmetic in every problem before printing. AI-generated maths is usually sound in its logic and unreliable in the third step of the calculation — this is the standing warning of the workshop the material comes from.
- The same worksheet works both as an introduction to the topic and as revision before a test; for revision, drop step 1.
- The material was created with ChatGPT and is published for reuse. Students do not use an AI tool in this lesson.
