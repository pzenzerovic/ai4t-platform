## Objective

Students can tell a need from a want, can plan a purchase within a set budget and can calculate how much money is left or how much is still missing.

## Preparation

- Printed worksheet, one per student (eight sections on one A3 sheet).
- Coloured pencils — the first task is solved by colouring.
- Pencils and erasers for the calculation tasks; no calculators.
- Optional: the class can be given real prices from a local shop leaflet as an extension of task 2.

## Lesson flow

1. **Need or want?** · 8 min — Students colour eight everyday items (bread, a video game, trainers, ice cream, a notebook, headphones, juice, a scooter) as either a need or a want, then justify two of their choices out loud. This sets the vocabulary for the whole lesson.
2. **Mini shopping challenge** · 12 min — Each student has a budget of €30 and a price list (pizza €8, headphones €18, notebook €3, chocolate €2, ball €15, juice €4, video game €25). They circle what they buy, add up the total spent and calculate what is left. Fast finishers try a second combination that leaves more money over.
3. **Smart or unsmart, saving, pocket money** · 12 min — Three linked tasks: judging five shopping decisions as smart or unsmart (buying five ice creams at once, saving part of the pocket money, buying school supplies, spending everything on the first day, comparing prices before buying); calculating how much Mia still needs for a €120 bicycle when she has €45; calculating how much Luka has after saving €5 a week for six weeks.
4. **My dream piggy bank and mini quiz** · 8 min — Students draw their own piggy bank and write what they are saving for, then answer three multiple-choice questions: what saving means, what a need is, and what is smart to do before buying.
5. **Super task: if you were given €50** · 5 min — Students split an imaginary €50 into three columns — saved, bought, gifted — and two or three of them present their split to the class. Closing message: spending wisely, saving and planning is how you reach your goals.

## Learning outcomes

- Students can classify everyday items as needs or wants and justify the classification.
- Students can plan a purchase within a given budget and calculate the remainder.
- Students can solve a subtraction word problem about saving (how much is still missing).
- Students can solve a multiplication word problem about regular saving over several weeks.
- Students can name at least two rules of smart spending.

## Notes

- Always check the arithmetic in AI-generated material before printing — this is the standing rule of the workshop the material comes from, and the price tasks here are exactly the kind that go wrong.
- The material was created with ChatGPT and is published for reuse. Students do not use an AI tool in this lesson.
- Task 2 can be repeated with the prices of items the class actually cares about; the structure stays the same.
