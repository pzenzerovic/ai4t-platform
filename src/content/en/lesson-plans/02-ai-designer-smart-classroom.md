## Objective

Students can write a prompt precise enough for an AI image tool to generate the classroom they imagined, and can explain why the wording of the prompt changes the result.

## Preparation

- Printed worksheet, one per student (8 sections, from the opening question to the class vote).
- Devices with access to Canva (Magic Media / Text to Image) — school computers or tablets; one device per student or per pair.
- Glue or a printer if students paste in the generated image, or coloured pencils if they draw it instead.
- The worksheet's three levels of prompt examples ready to show: *easier* (modern classroom of the future, smart desks, robots helping students), *medium* (futuristic eco classroom, AI assistant robot, smart lighting, hologram board), *fun* (space classroom on Mars, underwater classroom with glass walls).

## Lesson flow

1. **The best classroom in the world** · 5 min — Students write at least three ideas for what their smart classroom would have. Short exchange of ideas with the class.
2. **Meeting the tool** · 8 min — The teacher shows the six steps: open Canva → Apps → Magic Media → Text to Image → write the description (prompt) → the AI generates the image. The three levels of example prompts are read out and compared: what makes a description more precise?
3. **Become an AI designer** · 15 min — Each student writes their own prompt for a classroom that has at least three smart elements, a solution that helps students, an interesting appearance and a name. They generate the image, then paste or draw the result on the worksheet.
4. **Describe your classroom** · 7 min — Students answer: what is your classroom called, which three smart elements does it have, how does AI help students in it. Then the reflection questions: did the AI generate what you imagined (yes / partly / no), what was hardest to describe, can AI help designers and engineers, what did you learn today.
5. **The best classroom — class vote** · 5 min — The class votes in four categories (most creative, most realistic, most fun, the one I would like to learn in) and closes with a discussion of the role of AI in design and engineering.

## Learning outcomes

- Students can explain the basic purpose of artificial intelligence.
- Students can devise a prompt for an AI tool.
- Students can use an AI tool to generate content.
- Students can analyse the result they obtained and compare it with their intention.
- Students can connect artificial intelligence with technical occupations.

## Notes

- Reported from the piloting: the hardest part for students was writing a prompt precise enough. Through repeated attempts and revisions they produced better descriptions and better results — this is the pedagogical core of the lesson, not an obstacle to be avoided.
- The material builds on earlier workshops in which students learned how AI processes information, recognises patterns and learns from data.
- Recommendations from the report: add more prompt examples, compare different AI tools, and prepare extended activities for older students.
- The worksheet closes with the message that AI is an excellent tool, but a human must still think, plan and decide.
